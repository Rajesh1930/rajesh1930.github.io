Sheet 1 · Wind tunnel

DrivAer wind-tunnel model and CFD study

A 20% scale DrivAer model tested with three rear ends (fastback, notchback, estate) and two underbodies, then the same cases in CFD.

  • Led the team that CAD-modelled and FDM 3D-printed the model, with 62 surface-pressure taps.
  • Planned the test programme: forces, Reynolds-number sweeps, ±12° yaw and wool-tuft visualisation.
  • Built a 3.63-million-cell STAR-CCM+ model, mesh-independent to within 2% Cd.

What it taught me: the data is only as good as the model. Accurate geometry and repeatable configuration changes decide whether a 5% difference is real. Recommended next step: keep the detailed underbody and add a front splitter to bring lift towards neutral.

Sheet 2 · Race car aerodynamics

750 Formula aerodynamic package

My MSc dissertation: reduce the drag of a real 750 Motor Club race car with rule-compliant CAD changes, verifying each one in CFD.

  • Found and reviewed 452 problem regions in the supplied CAD before meshing.
  • Ran a 13-level mesh study (1.1 to 7.6 million cells) and chose a 6.4-million-cell mesh.
  • Designed a single-element NACA 0012 rear wing, diffuser and wheel-arch inlets in CATIA V5, within the class size and height limits, one change at a time.
−9.9%Cd, 0.537 to 0.484
771 → 708 NDrag force at 50 m/s
The trade-off: downforce fell by about 47% (|Cl| 0.427 to 0.225). This is a drag-optimised design, not a proven lap-time gain. Next step: minimise drag at a fixed minimum downforce and axle balance.
Vehicle750 Formula
Mesh6,385,896 cells
ToolsCATIA V5, STAR-CCM+
